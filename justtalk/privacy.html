Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026. Applies to the JustTalk apps for macOS and Windows, the JustTalk iOS app and its keyboard, and this website.

JustTalk has no analytics, no crash reporter, no tracking and no account with us. It does not phone home. It does, in normal use, send your speech to a cloud transcription and polishing service that you choose and hold the key for — so most of what matters here is that provider's policy, not this one. The on-device option sends nothing anywhere, but it is the exception rather than the default.

What stays on your device

All of it, unless you pick a cloud backend. Stored locally and never uploaded by JustTalk:

  • Your dictation history, including the transcribed text
  • Modes, your custom dictionary and quick actions
  • Usage statistics — words dictated, time saved, estimated spend
  • Recorded audio, which is held in memory for the length of a dictation and not written to disk
  • API keys and sign-in tokens for the services you configure — in the Keychain on Mac and iPhone, in Windows Credential Manager on Windows
  • Whisper models you download, if you use on-device transcription

Deleting the app on iPhone or uninstalling it on Windows deletes its data; on the Mac, deleting the app's Application Support folder does. Keys saved on Windows stay in Credential Manager until you remove them there. JustTalk has no server to delete anything from.

Purchases

On Mac and iPhone, JustTalk Pro is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple. The App Store tells the app whether it is entitled; it never sees your name, your email or your payment details. A Windows licence is not on sale yet. When you request one, the request you send us by email carries the name and email address you type into it and a one-way hash of your PC's hardware identifiers, so that the key works on that PC. We use them to issue and re-issue your key and for nothing else.

Both App Store purchases are Family Shareable. If a member of your family group shares one with you, Apple tells the app it is entitled and nothing else about them.

The iPhone keyboard

The JustTalk keyboard asks for Allow Full Access, which is the permission iOS requires before a keyboard may reach the network or share anything with its own app. Here is exactly what it does with it:

  • It does not read what is already in the field. It types, and it keeps track of what it typed so that undo and the quick actions can work on that text and nothing else.
  • It does not log what you type. Keys you press go into the app you are typing into and nowhere else.
  • It never sends anything anywhere itself. iOS does not let a keyboard open the microphone, so the JustTalk app records — and it is the app, not the keyboard, that talks to the service you configured.
  • While a session is running, the JustTalk app holds the microphone and iOS lights the orange indicator for the whole time. That is honest: the microphone genuinely is available to you until you stop the session.
  • Dictation on the iPhone is transcribed on the device by default, which sends nothing at all. Choosing a provider in Speech is what sends your audio anywhere.
  • Your dictation history is off unless you switch it on, and is encrypted on the device when it is on.

When you choose a cloud backend

JustTalk can send audio, or the transcript of it, to a provider you configure — Google, OpenAI, Groq, Mistral, Anthropic, or a server address you enter yourself. That data is then handled under their privacy policy, not this one, and JustTalk has no visibility into what they keep. The app shows which provider is active before you record, and switching to on-device transcription stops any of it from leaving your device.

What is sent alongside your words

When a polishing model is in use, JustTalk may include the name of the app you are dictating into so the result fits the context. You can turn that off with Privacy Mode, which stops any app or window information being used or sent.

Children

JustTalk is a productivity tool for adults, not a children's app. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Required-reason APIs

JustTalk uses the microphone — and on Mac and Windows, system audio when you record a meeting. macOS and iOS ask your permission first; on Windows, the microphone settings under Privacy & security decide. On iPhone it also asks for speech recognition, which on-device transcription keeps on the phone. It reads no contacts, no location, no photos and no health data. Apple's privacy questionnaire for the listings is answered from exactly this page.

This website

These pages are static files served by GitHub Pages. They set no cookies, embed no scripts and run no analytics. GitHub may log requests as part of serving them — see GitHub's privacy statement.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the date at the top changes with it. A change that reduced your privacy would come with a new version of the app, not a quiet edit here.
