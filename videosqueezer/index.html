Smaller files that still look like your videos

Judge it with your own eyes

Most of what makes a phone video huge is data you will never see. VideoSqueezer uses efficient HEVC, keeps the resolution and frame rate, and adapts to each clip, because a sunset and a football match compress very differently.

Don't take our word for it. Drag the split across the same frame, hold to magnify at real pixel scale, and decide before a single original is touched.

Space back, without the fear

Nothing gone for good

Cleaner apps love a big red Delete button. VideoSqueezer does not. Originals from Photos wait in Recently Deleted for 30 days; originals from Files are held where you can put them back.

And the number it shows you is honest: space you actually freed is kept apart from space you merely could.

Questions people ask

Will my videos look worse?

At Maximum Quality the difference is not visible; at Balanced it is very close to the original. You can compare before and after on the same frame before committing.

How much space will I get back?

It depends on the video. Space Radar shows the estimate for each one before anything is changed — 4K clips shot on an iPhone often shrink the most.

Does it upload my videos?

No. Everything happens on your device. There is no server, no account and no analytics.

What happens to the originals?

Nothing, until you say so. Originals from Photos go to Recently Deleted for 30 days; originals from Files are held where you can put them back.

Is it a subscription?

Only if you want one. There is a single purchase that unlocks everything for good, and a yearly option. Clips up to 60 seconds are always free.

Why does the app stay open while it works?

iOS stops video encoding about thirty seconds after an app leaves the screen. So there is a screen built for it: it keeps the queue going and pauses itself if the phone gets warm.
